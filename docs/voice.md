# Website voice

Write like the person who ran the experiment explaining what happened to someone
choosing a model. Be curious, direct and willing to be checked. The reader should
be able to hear a person and inspect the evidence behind what that person says.

## What earns the tone

- Own our choices. Say what we tested, why we counted it that way and what we have
  not tested yet. Use “we” for our work and “you” for the reader's next action.
- Put a concrete fact behind a claim of openness: downloadable responses, pinned
  inputs, an unchanged release or a command that checks the scores.
- Explain our limits where they affect a decision. A test score can help someone
  choose what to try; it cannot promise fluency or success on their own tasks.
- Be definite about what we did and precise about what we can infer. Statistical
  uncertainty stays in the copy. Removing a caveat does not make a claim honest.
- Let humor come from the work: wrong answers, missing results, the effort of
  checking things. A reader's language, ability or question is never the joke.

## Writing and editing

Use familiar words and active verbs. Explain what happened before naming the
machinery. Keep technical names, units and conditions wherever they help someone
interpret a result or run a test. Controls should say what they do.

A short aside can give a headline or invitation some personality. Instructions,
errors, score labels and uncertainty explanations should be straightforward.
Read copy aloud; remove anything you would feel odd saying to the reader.
Swearing, internet jokes and deliberately awkward grammar are not requirements.

Avoid inflated promises, generic excitement, sales pressure and invented company
policies. Do not claim complete language or model coverage, a universal winner,
independence from funding, or a guarantee of reproducible live model answers.
Dataset and language support come from the available adapters, not our slogans.

The benchmark's [identity and scoring rules](protocol.md) still govern every
sentence. In particular, never imply a required language pair or confuse a
homepage average with an individual experiment's paired uncertainty interval.

## English and Russian

Keep facts, supported actions, numerical values and qualifications identical.
Translate the intent and conversational rhythm rather than individual jokes.
Use natural, respectful Russian with “вы”; avoid bureaucratic phrasing and forced
English slang. Keep ICU arguments and rich-text tags intact in both dictionaries.
Review each version in its actual layout, including narrow screens.
